AI Businesses: from worst to best for getting started

Thirteen businesses you can start with AI today, with INEMA’s scores for technical demands, market opportunity, and durability — and a path to move from one to the next.

See the rankingTake the assessment
In brief

Every day, a new business idea built around AI appears, and choosing has become harder than building. This page evaluates thirteen of them with three scores from 1 to 5: how much technical knowledge you need to deliver something someone will pay for, how much room there is in the market (more buyers than sellers), and how long the business can last and grow without being swallowed by a platform. The list ranks them from worst to best for people just starting out. The rule that emerges at the end is simple: at the bottom, you sell what you build, which gets cheaper every month; at the top, you sell your judgment, which gets more valuable. That’s why the second part is a four-step ladder, with a 6-question assessment to find your step, a ready-to-use offer for each business, industry examples, and a 90-day plan. The prices in Brazilian reais are reference ranges for what people charge, not a promise of earnings.

How we scored them

Three scores, from 1 to 5

None of the businesses on this list are bad: they all work for someone. The scores show what kind of challenge you’ll face.

Technical demands

How much knowledge you need to deliver something someone will pay for. A high score means it’s harder.

Market opportunity

Whether more people want to buy than are currently selling. A high score means more opportunity.

Durability and growth

Whether it can become a business that lasts for years without being swallowed by a platform. A high score means it’s more resilient.

13ºStep 1

AI subscription app (SaaS)

In a nutshellThe most talked-about and the hardest to start: building the app is easy; getting people to pay every month isn’t.
How it works

You build software with help from AI and charge a monthly subscription for people to use it. The potential is enormous, it can grow with a small team, and that’s why it’s the business that shows up most often in success stories.

The problem is that building has become the cheap part. Thousands of apps launch every week, but people’s attention hasn’t grown. The real business is getting discovered, convincing people to pay, and preventing cancellations. As usage grows, security and infrastructure costs add up. And an app that’s just a shell around a model can die with the platform’s next update.

What you can charge (reference)R$ 29 to R$ 299 per month per client; you need hundreds of clients to make a living from it.
Who it’s forPeople who already have an audience, a service business, or enough cash to get through months without revenue.
The pitfallBuilding before you have buyers. Most SaaS businesses that grow started as a service that was already selling.
The offer in one sentence“I handle [task] for [type of company] for a monthly fee, with nothing for you to install.”

12ºStep 1

Faceless channel made with AI

In a nutshellCheap to start and great for learning, but views alone don’t pay the bills.
How it works

A YouTube, TikTok, or Instagram account where AI helps create the content and you don’t appear on camera. The startup cost is almost zero, and you can test several formats in a short time.

The money doesn’t come from views; it comes from people who want to reach that audience: sponsors, your own product, or a paid community. Mass-produced repetitive content isn’t monetized by platforms, and viral content without a defined audience won’t attract anyone willing to pay. First think about who watches and who would pay to reach them.

What you can charge (reference)Sponsorships from R$ 300 to R$ 5.000 per post when the audience is niche and engaged.
Who it’s forPeople who want to learn to create with AI and are willing to build an audience for months before earning money.
The pitfallChoosing a topic based on what goes viral instead of who would pay to reach that audience. And the platform can change the rules overnight.
The offer in one sentence“My channel talks to [audience] every day; your product reaches them for [price].”

11ºStep 1

AI-built websites for businesses

In a nutshellThe best place for a beginner to start: quick, visual, and easy to show. But there’s a low ceiling.
How it works

You build websites for small businesses in an afternoon using AI tools instead of taking weeks. You can put together a demo of the client’s own website before talking to them, which changes the conversation: they see it finished before paying.

Agencies still charge a lot for the old way of doing things, so you can enter at a lower price and deliver faster. The limit is that AI website builders cost just a few dozen reais a month, and the client will find out. Use this to build momentum, a portfolio, and your first clients, then move up.

What you can charge (reference)R$ 1.500 to R$ 5.000 per website, plus R$ 100 to R$ 300 per month for maintenance.
Who it’s forComplete beginners who want a first client quickly and something visual to show.
The pitfallSelling only websites. It’s hard to get past a few thousand reais a month.
The offer in one sentence“I made a new version of your website; if you like it, I can publish it this week.”

10ºStep 2

Company AI hub

In a nutshellA great way to get inside a company and build trust, but hard to connect to a financial result.
How it works

You bring the agents, automations, tools, and team knowledge together in one place: an assistant that knows the documents and processes and responds through the channel the company already uses.

It’s an excellent entry point because once clients trust you, they’ll buy larger projects. The weak point is measurement. Every offer needs a number the owner tracks (sales, hours, customer interactions), and a generic hub doesn’t move a clear number. It also only works if the owner already believes in AI; otherwise, the team won’t change its habits and you won’t have a case study.

What you can charge (reference)R$ 5.000 to R$ 20.000 for setup, plus R$ 800 to R$ 3.000 per month.
Who it’s forPeople who already know agents and automations and have access to tech-friendly business owners.
The pitfallSelling the hub without tying it to a number. Without measured results, there are no referrals.
The offer in one sentence“I put everything your team asks about into one assistant, and response time is cut in half.”

9ºStep 2

Show up in AI answers (GEO)

In a nutshellIt’s SEO for the ChatGPT era: money is coming in and few people are doing it, but you need to know how to prove results.
How it works

You work to get a client’s brand recommended when someone asks ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, or Perplexity a question. It’s an extension of SEO, a market that has existed for decades and that companies know they need to pay for.

People who already do SEO have a major advantage because they know how to measure and show results. Without that foundation, it’s hard to prove the change came from your work. And AI companies have started selling ads, which could change the rules about who shows up.

What you can charge (reference)R$ 2.000 to R$ 6.000 per month per client.
Who it’s forPeople who already work in SEO, content, or digital marketing.
The pitfallPromising a position in an AI answer. Promise the work and show the measured progress.
The offer in one sentence“Today, AI recommends your competitor when people ask about [service]. In 90 days, I’ll change that, and you can track the results.”

8ºStep 2

Automation and integration

In a nutshellDemand keeps growing, but ready-made workflows have become a commodity. The value is in finding the bottleneck.
How it works

You connect AI to the systems the company already uses: CRM, email, spreadsheets, invoices, WhatsApp. It’s where many people started and is still where service requests come from most often.

But building automations is getting easier and easier, and many companies already hire someone internally to do it. A one-off workflow is worth little. What matters is finding the most expensive bottleneck, tying the fix to a business result, and charging for that result — and that’s already the consulting step.

What you can charge (reference)R$ 1.500 to R$ 6.000 per workflow; more when charging based on results.
Who it’s forPeople who like logic and tools and want a service with steady demand.
The pitfallSelling one workflow at a time and competing on price with thousands of people.
The offer in one sentence“Your team spends [X] hours a week on [task]. I’ll take that off their hands in 15 days.”

7ºStep 3

AI workshops for teams

In a nutshellYou get paid to teach and leave the room as the company’s “AI person.”
How it works

You teach a company’s team how to use AI in each person’s work, using their real cases. Most employees say they haven’t received proper training, and companies know it.

The bigger payoff comes later. In the room, you see the company’s problems and build trust immediately. A larger project often follows the workshop. Teaching is a skill you can practice, and you need to master the tools you teach.

What you can charge (reference)R$ 3.000 for a half day to R$ 15.000 for a full day with a large group.
Who it’s forPeople who like explaining things, speak well in public, and already use AI in their own work.
The pitfallA generic “ChatGPT for beginners” workshop. Bring in the company’s own cases, or no one will use it the next day.
The offer in one sentence“In 3 hours, your team will leave using AI for [5 industry tasks], with materials to keep going.”

6ºStep 2

AI customer service and receptionist

In a nutshellAI that handles WhatsApp, phone calls, and websites, books appointments, and hands off to a human. In Brazil, WhatsApp makes this business a top opportunity.
How it works

AI responds to messages, phone calls, and website chat, answers questions, books appointments, and brings in a person when needed. The return is easy to explain: many calls and messages to small businesses go unanswered, and for a clinic, repair shop, or office, each one is a lost client.

In Brazil, where almost everything goes through WhatsApp, the pain is even more obvious. The risk is durability: the scheduling and customer service systems these businesses already pay for are adding their own AI. The winners will be people who know the industry and take care of setup, rules, and ongoing support.

What you can charge (reference)R$ 2.000 to R$ 8.000 for setup, plus R$ 500 to R$ 2.000 per month.
Who it’s forPeople who want a service with recurring monthly revenue and results that are easy to show the owner.
The pitfallSelling the same generic AI to everyone. The system the client already uses will offer the same thing for less.
The offer in one sentence“None of your messages go unanswered, even at 10 p.m.; AI books the appointment and calls you when needed.”

5ºStep 2

AI creative studio

In a nutshellAds, videos, and carousels for brands. Anyone can hit “generate”; clients pay for taste and results.
How it works

You create ads, commercials, carousels, and video edits for brands using AI. One person can do today what used to require a team and expensive equipment, and AI video editing is among the most in-demand skills.

The flip side is that anyone can hit the generate button, including inside the ad tools themselves, and the price per piece is falling. Clients pay for taste, for knowing which model works for which audience, and for using each ad’s numbers to make the next one better. If your ads sell, the client stays.

What you can charge (reference)R$ 2.000 to R$ 8.000 per month per brand, in a package of creative assets.
Who it’s forPeople with a good eye for visuals who like video and want to track ad performance.
The pitfallSelling individual pretty assets. Sell a monthly package tied to campaign results.
The offer in one sentence“Every month, you get [N] new ads, and I replace the ones that don’t sell with ones that do.”

4ºStep 3

AI-powered lead generation

In a nutshellThe clearest result on the list: more leads, more sales. If it works, no one cancels.
How it works

You use AI to find companies and people showing buying signals, enrich the data, personalize outreach, and test far more messages than a human team could. You deliver meetings or qualified leads to the client.

It’s the “stickiest” business: if you bring in clients and your client makes money from them, they won’t cancel. The metric is the easiest to measure. The requirement is tough: you need to generate your own clients first, or no one will believe you can generate theirs. People who already have a prospecting agency and add AI have a head start.

What you can charge (reference)R$ 3.000 to R$ 10.000 per month, or a fee per meeting booked.
Who it’s forPeople who like sales, numbers, and testing, and are willing to prove the method on themselves.
The pitfallMass outreach without a clear purpose. It burns the domain, the client’s WhatsApp number, and their reputation.
The offer in one sentence“I’ll put [N] meetings with [type of client] on your calendar each month. You pay for what happens.”

3ºStep 4

Education and personal brand

In a nutshellThe most durable of all: people who trust you follow you when the tools change. But it takes years.
How it works

Courses, community, newsletters, and videos, preferably with your face and voice. You need to work with the subject every day to teach it, or at least feel comfortable on camera. Competition is growing, but so is the audience that wants to learn.

It’s the most durable business because people invest in you, not a tool: when the technology changes, you change with it and they stay. The problem is the timeline. Building an audience takes years, and trust comes from proof. That’s why the common path is to provide services and consulting first, then use your brand to launch the other businesses.

What you can charge (reference)From one-off courses to a community subscription; it depends on the size and trust of the audience.
Who it’s forPeople with hands-on experience to show, who enjoy teaching and can keep building over time.
The pitfallStarting with a brand before you have proof. First results, then trust, then an audience.
The offer in one sentence“Every week, I show how to solve [problem] with AI, using real cases.”

2ºStep 3

AI consulting and diagnosis

In a nutshellYou’re the doctor: learn how the company works, find the bottleneck, and prescribe where AI will save the most.
How it works

You go into a company, understand its processes, find the bottleneck, and identify where AI can save the most time or money. Fixes get cheaper every month; diagnosis still has value. And there’s the human part no AI can solve: convincing the team, setting usage rules, and helping people change their habits.

AI companies themselves have opened service divisions to deploy their tools in businesses, a sign that implementation is where the work is. Consulting pays sooner than education and opens the door to automation, customer service, and training projects.

What you can charge (reference)Diagnosis from R$ 3.000 to R$ 30.000, plus implementation.
Who it’s forPeople who understand business, ask good questions, and have already delivered a few AI projects.
The pitfallDelivering a polished report and leaving. Clients pay for change, not for a PDF.
The offer in one sentence“In one week, I’ll show you where AI can save your company the most, in reais, and implement the first opportunity.”

1ºStep 4

AI director (in-house or fractional)

In a nutshellThe top step: take responsibility for a company’s AI strategy, or several companies’ strategies at once.
How it works

There are two equally good paths. Become the AI lead at the company where you already work and take responsibility for its strategy and results. Or work as a fractional director, serving a few companies at once for a few hours a week each. This is often the natural next step for someone who has done consulting.

Large companies are creating this role quickly, and professionals with AI skills already earn more than their peers. It requires knowing enough not to be caught off guard and carrying yourself like an executive. The further AI advances, the more valuable it becomes to decide how to use it.

What you can charge (reference)Fractional: R$ 8.000 to R$ 25.000 per month per company. In-house: director-level salary.
Who it’s forPeople with management or consulting experience and the trust of business owners and directors.
The pitfallWanting the title without proof. It comes after measured results at other companies.
The offer in one sentence“I manage your company’s AI a few hours a week: strategy, tools, team, and results.”

What the list teaches

At the bottom, you sell what you build. At the top, you sell your judgment.

Building websites, automations, or apps gets cheaper every month because AI does much of the work. Diagnosing the right problem, convincing a team to change, and being accountable for the result remain expensive because they depend on you. Starting at the bottom isn’t a mistake: that’s where you gain experience and your first clients. The mistake is staying there.
