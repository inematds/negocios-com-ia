AI Businesses: from worst to best — and where to start

Thirteen businesses you can start today with AI, ranked from worst to best for building, with INEMA’s scores for technical demands, market opportunity, and durability — plus a ladder showing where to enter and how to climb.

See the rankingTake the assessment
In brief

Every day, a new business idea built around artificial intelligence appears, and choosing has become harder than building. This page rates thirteen of them on three 1-to-5 scales: how much technical knowledge you need to deliver something people will pay for, how much room there is in the market (more buyers than sellers), and how long the business can last and grow without being swallowed by a platform. The list ranks them from worst to best for building over the long term. That is not the same as the best one to start with: the top spot requires experience no one has on day one, and some businesses near the bottom are great first steps. The simple rule at the end is this: at the bottom, you sell what you build, which gets cheaper every month; at the top, you sell your judgment, which becomes more valuable. That is why the second part is a four-step ladder, with a 6-question assessment to find your step, a ready-to-use offer for each business, industry examples, and a 90-day plan. Prices in reais are reference ranges for what businesses charge, not a promise of earnings.

How we rate them

Three scores, from 1 to 5

None of the businesses on this list is bad: each works for someone. The scores show what kind of challenges you will face. The ranking is not the sum of the scores: durability and whether the business takes you to the next step carry more weight.

Technical demands

How much knowledge you need to deliver something people will pay for. A high score means it is harder.

Market opportunity

Whether more people want to buy than are selling today. A high score means more room to grow.

Durability and growth

Whether it can become a business that lasts for years without being swallowed by a platform. A high score means it is more resilient.

🪜 The step is a different measure: it shows how much proof and trust you need to sell that business. That is why the list and ladder have different orders — the ladder helps you find where to enter. Ladder →

13ºStep 1

AI subscription app (SaaS)

In one sentenceThe most talked-about and hardest to start: building the app has become easy; getting people to pay every month has not.
How it works

You build software with AI and charge people a monthly fee to use it. The potential is enormous, it can grow with a small team, and that is why it is the business that shows up most in success stories.

The problem is that building has become the cheap part. It has never been easier to launch an app, which means more competitors are fighting for the same attention. The real work is getting discovered, convincing people to pay, and keeping them from canceling. As usage grows, security and infrastructure costs add up. And an app that is just a thin layer over a model can die with the platform’s next update.

Typical pricing (reference)R$ 29 to R$ 299 per month per client; you need hundreds of clients to make a living from it.
Who it suitsPeople who already have an audience, a service business, or enough cash to go months without revenue.
The trapBuilding before you have buyers. Most SaaS businesses that grow started as a service that was already selling.
The offer in one sentence“I solve [task] for [type of business] for a monthly fee, with nothing for you to install.”

12ºStep 1

Faceless channel made with AI

In one sentenceCheap to start and good for learning, but views alone do not pay the bills.
How it works

A YouTube, TikTok, or Instagram account where AI helps create the content and you stay off camera. Startup costs are almost zero, and you can test several formats quickly.

The money does not come from views; it comes from people who want to reach that audience: sponsors, your own products, or a paid community. Platforms do not monetize mass-produced repetitive content, and viral content without a defined audience does not attract anyone willing to pay. Start by thinking about who watches and who would pay to reach them.

Typical pricing (reference)Sponsorships of R$ 300 to R$ 5,000 per post when the audience is engaged and focused on a niche.
Who it suitsPeople who want to learn to create with AI and are willing to build an audience for months before earning money.
The trapChoosing a topic for its viral potential instead of thinking about who would pay to reach that audience. The platform can also change its rules overnight.
The offer in one sentence“My channel reaches [audience] every day; your product gets in front of them for [price].”

11ºStep 1

AI-built websites for businesses

In one sentenceThe best place for beginners to start: fast, visual, and easy to show. But there is a low ceiling.
How it works

You build websites for small businesses with AI tools in an afternoon instead of taking weeks. You can create a demo of the client’s own website before speaking with them, which changes the conversation: they see it finished before paying.

Agencies still charge high prices for the old way of doing things, so you can enter at a lower price and deliver faster. The limit is that AI website builders cost just a few dozen reais a month, and the client will find out. Use this business to build momentum, a portfolio, and your first clients, then move up.

Typical pricing (reference)R$ 1,500 to R$ 5,000 per website, plus R$ 100 to R$ 300 per month for maintenance.
Who it suitsComplete beginners who want a first client quickly and something visual to show.
The trapSelling only websites. It is hard to get past a few thousand reais a month.
The offer in one sentence“I made a new version of your website; if you like it, I can publish it this week.”

10ºStep 2

Company-wide AI hub

In one sentenceA great way to get inside a company and earn trust, but hard to tie to a financial result.
How it works

You bring the company’s agents, automations, tools, and team knowledge together in one place: an assistant that knows its documents and processes and responds through the channel the company already uses.

It is an excellent entry point because once a client trusts you, they will buy larger projects. The weak point is measurement. Every offer needs a metric the owner tracks (sales, hours, service interactions), and a generic hub does not move a clear metric. It also only works if the owner already believes in AI; otherwise, the team will not change its habits and you will have no case study.

Typical pricing (reference)R$ 5,000 to R$ 20,000 to set up, plus R$ 800 to R$ 3,000 per month.
Who it suitsPeople who already know agents and automations and have access to tech-minded business owners.
The trapSelling the hub without tying it to a metric. Without measured results, you will not get referrals.
The offer in one sentence“I bring everything your team asks about into one assistant, and we measure how much response time drops.”

9ºStep 2

Get your brand into AI answers (GEO)

In one sentenceSEO for the ChatGPT era: money is coming in and few people are doing it, but you need to know how to prove results.
How it works

You work to get a client’s brand recommended when someone asks ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, or Perplexity a question. It is the next step in SEO (the work of getting a website to show up on Google), a market that has existed for decades and that companies know they need to pay for.

People who already do SEO have a huge advantage because they know how to measure and show results. Without that foundation, it is hard to prove the change came from your work. And the rules for which brands appear in answers can change at any time.

Typical pricing (reference)R$ 2,000 to R$ 6,000 per month per client.
Who it suitsPeople who already work in SEO, content, or digital marketing.
The trapPromising a spot in an AI answer. Promise the work and show measured progress.
The offer in one sentence“Today, AI recommends your competitor when people ask about [service]. I work to change that and show you the measurements every month.”

8ºStep 2

Automation and integration

In one sentenceDemand keeps growing, but ready-made workflows have become a commodity. The value lies in finding the bottleneck.
How it works

You connect AI to the systems a company already uses: its customer management system (CRM), email, spreadsheets, invoicing, and WhatsApp. It is where many people started, and it is still where service requests are most common.

But building automations is getting easier, and many companies already hire someone in-house to do it. A standalone workflow is worth little. What matters is finding the most costly bottleneck, tying the fix to a business result, and charging for that result — and that is already the consulting step.

Typical pricing (reference)R$ 1,500 to R$ 6,000 per workflow; more when priced based on results.
Who it suitsPeople who enjoy logic and tools and want a service with steady demand.
The trapSelling one workflow at a time and competing on price with thousands of people.
The offer in one sentence“Your team spends [X] hours a week on [task]. I automate it and show you the hours given back.”

7ºStep 3

AI workshops for teams

In one sentenceYou get paid to teach and leave the room as “the AI person” at the company.
How it works

You teach a company’s team to use AI in their daily work, using the company’s real cases. At many companies, the team already uses AI on its own without training, and the owner knows it.

The bigger payoff comes afterward. In the room, you see the company’s problems and earn trust right away. A larger project often follows the workshop. Teaching is a skill you can practice, and you need to know the tools you teach.

Typical pricing (reference)R$ 3,000 for a half day to R$ 15,000 for a full day with a large group.
Who it suitsPeople who enjoy explaining things, speak well in public, and already use AI in their own work.
The trapA generic “ChatGPT for beginners” workshop. Bring the company’s own cases, or nobody will use it the next day.
The offer in one sentence“In 3 hours, your team will start using AI for [5 tasks in your industry], with materials to keep going.”

6ºStep 2

AI receptionist and customer service agent

In one sentenceAI that handles WhatsApp, phone, and website inquiries, books appointments, and hands off to a human. In Brazil, WhatsApp puts this business near the top.
How it works

AI responds to messages, phone calls, and website chats, answers questions, books appointments, and calls a person when needed. It is easy to explain the return: the owner only has to count how many messages and calls went unanswered last month, and for a clinic, repair shop, or office, each one could be a lost client.

In Brazil, where almost everything goes through WhatsApp, the pain is even more visible. The risk is durability: scheduling and customer service systems these businesses already pay for are adding their own AI. The winners will be the people who know the industry and take care of setup, rules, and ongoing support.

Typical pricing (reference)R$ 2,000 to R$ 8,000 to set up, plus R$ 500 to R$ 2,000 per month.
Who it suitsPeople who want a recurring service and results that are easy for the owner to see.
The trapSelling the same generic AI to everyone. The system the client already uses will offer the same thing for less.
The offer in one sentence“Your clinic responds to every message, even at 10 p.m.: AI books the appointment and calls you when needed.”

5ºStep 2

AI creative studio

In one sentenceAds, videos, and carousels for brands. Anyone can press “generate”; clients pay for taste and results.
How it works

You produce ads, commercials, carousels, and video edits for brands with AI. One person can now do what used to require a team and expensive equipment.

The other side is that anyone can press the generate button, including inside the ad platforms themselves, and the price per asset is falling. Clients pay for taste, for knowing which model suits which audience, and for using each ad’s numbers to make the next one better. If your ads sell, the client will stay.

Typical pricing (reference)R$ 2,000 to R$ 8,000 per month per brand, packaged by deliverables.
Who it suitsPeople with a good eye for visuals who enjoy video and want to track ad performance.
The trapSelling attractive assets one at a time. Sell a monthly package tied to campaign results.
The offer in one sentence“Every month, you get [N] new ads, and I replace the ones that do not sell with ones that do.”

4ºStep 3

AI lead generation

In one sentenceThe clearest result on the list: more leads, more sales. If it works, nobody cancels.
How it works

You use AI to find businesses and people showing buying signals (an open position, a new store, an expansion), fill in their contact details, personalize your outreach, and test far more messages than a human team could. You deliver meetings or genuinely interested leads to the client.

It is the “stickiest” business: if you bring in clients and the client makes money from them, they will not cancel. The metric is the easiest to measure. But the requirement is tough: you need to generate your own clients first, or nobody will believe you can generate theirs. People who already run a prospecting agency and add AI have a head start.

Typical pricing (reference)R$ 3,000 to R$ 10,000 per month, or a fee per meeting booked.
Who it suitsPeople who enjoy sales, numbers, and testing, and are willing to prove the method on themselves.
The trapMass outreach without targeting. It can send the client’s email and WhatsApp number to spam or get them blocked, damaging their reputation.
The offer in one sentence“I find [type of client] for your calendar, and you pay for the meetings that happen.”

3ºStep 4

Education and personal brand

In one sentenceThe most durable of all: people who trust you will follow you when the tools change. But it takes years.
How it works

Courses, communities, newsletters, and videos, ideally featuring your face and voice. You need to work with the subject day to day to teach it, or at least feel comfortable on camera. Competition is growing, but so is the number of people who want to learn.

It is the most durable business because people invest in you, not in a tool: when technology changes, you change with it and they stay. The problem is speed. Building an audience takes years, and trust comes from proof. That is why a common path is to offer services and consulting first, then build your brand to launch the other businesses.

Typical pricing (reference)From one-off courses to a community subscription; it depends on the audience’s size and trust.
Who it suitsPeople with experience to share who enjoy teaching and are willing to build gradually.
The trapStarting with a brand before you have proof. First results, then trust, then an audience.
The offer in one sentence“Every week, I show you how to solve [problem] with AI, using real cases.”

2ºStep 3

AI consulting and diagnostics

In one sentenceYou are the doctor: learn how the company works, find the bottleneck, and prescribe where AI will save the most.
How it works

You go into the company, understand its processes, find the bottleneck, and recommend where AI can save the most time or money. Fixes get cheaper every month; diagnosis still has value. And there is the human part no AI can handle: persuading the team, setting usage rules, and helping people change their habits.

Implementing AI properly in a company takes work, and most of the value lies in that implementation. Consulting pays off faster than education and creates opportunities for automation, customer service, and training projects.

Typical pricing (reference)A diagnostic engagement costs R$ 3,000 to R$ 30,000, plus implementation.
Who it suitsPeople who understand business, ask good questions, and have already delivered a few AI projects.
The trapHanding over a polished report and leaving. The client pays for change, not for a PDF.
The offer in one sentence“In one week, I will show you where AI can save the most in your company, in reais, and implement the first solution.”

1ºStep 4

AI Director (in-house or fractional)

In one sentenceThe top role: take responsibility for a company’s AI strategy, or for several companies at once.
How it works

There are two equally good paths. Become the AI lead at the company where you already work, and take responsibility for strategy and results. Or work as a fractional director, serving a few companies at a time for several hours a week each. This is often the natural next step for someone who has done consulting.

More and more companies want someone accountable for AI, and people who combine AI knowledge with business perspective tend to be more valued. It requires knowing enough to avoid being caught off guard and carrying yourself like an executive. As AI advances, people who decide how to use it become more valuable.

Typical pricing (reference)Fractional: R$ 8,000 to R$ 25,000 per month per company. In-house: executive salary.
Who it suitsPeople with management or consulting experience and the trust of owners and directors.
The trapWanting the title without the proof. It comes after you have delivered measured results at other companies.
The offer in one sentence“I manage your company’s AI a few hours a week: strategy, tools, team, and results.”

What the list teaches

At the bottom, you sell what you build. At the top, you sell your judgment.

Building a website, automation, or app gets cheaper every month because AI does much of the work. Diagnosing the right problem, persuading a team to change, and taking responsibility for the outcome remain expensive because they depend on you. Starting at the bottom is not a mistake: that is where you gain experience and your first clients. The mistake is staying there.
